Executive summary

1.1What POC F is

POC F measures how fast the real questions are at scale, under each pricing set-up, with and without security, and works out the monthly cost and the break-even point. Plan The plan describes it as "a test drive with a fuel bill": the figures come from a generated workload, so they show how to decide, not GKS's real bill.

At the end of POC F (plan, section 11):

  1. scale holds about 50 million alert-history rows, with the same schema, partitioning, clustering and row policies as the warehouse.
  2. Ten benchmark questions are run in every cell of a matrix of pricing set-ups and identities.
  3. docs/poc2/benchmark-results.md and docs/poc2/cost-model.md are written.
  4. A Looker Studio report shows different rows to two tenant users.
  5. Both reservations (BI Engine and Enterprise) are destroyed and scale is deleted.

1.2What this document is

One technical reference for POC F. It follows the approved plan (GKS-POC-Phase2-detailed-plan.md, v2.0, 17 Nov 2026) and adds the cost mathematics the plan only outlines: formulas, worked examples, a break-even derivation and a Pricing Calculator procedure. It does not replace the two POC F deliverables (benchmark-results.md and cost-model.md); it is the method those documents will follow.

1.4Three findings that shape the rest

  1. The free monthly query allowance decides the break-even. On-demand costs nothing below the allowance, so editions (slot-hours) cannot be cheaper until monthly volume passes it. Section 14 derives the exact point.
  2. A small query still bills a minimum. In the dev project, 2,615 query jobs were each billed exactly 10 MiB (10,485,760 bytes). At dashboard volume that floor alone is 0.29 TiB a month (section 10). Measured Derived
  3. Time is the biggest lever on the always-on parts. The night-off workflows cut running hours to about a third. Cost items that bill by the hour (Cloud SQL, Cloud Run, Workflows executions) fall by about the same share; storage does not (section 13).

F6 Benchmark runs (Dev 1, Dev 5, days 11–12)

F7 Cost model (Dev 1, day 12)

  1. Measure the workload from region-us-central1.INFORMATION_SCHEMA.JOBS_BY_PROJECT: one normal day of micro-batch runs (Day 9) and the benchmark queries.
  2. Define three monthly scenarios: S1 the POC workload running all the time plus 20 dashboard users × 50 queries a day; S2 = S1 with 10× the data; S3 = S1 with 100× the data.
  3. Take prices from the BigQuery pricing page on the day, with the date written down: on-demand per TiB, Enterprise per slot-hour, BI Engine per GiB-hour, storage. Never in code.
  4. On-demand cost = TiB billed × price, after the free monthly allowance.
  5. Editions cost = slot-hours × price, where slot-hours = SUM(total_slot_ms) / 3.6e6, plus an allowance for autoscaling steps and the one-minute minimum, plus any baseline × 730 hours.
  6. Break-even: the monthly TiB at which the two are equal, shown for each scenario.
  7. State that the figures are based on a generated workload and list every assumption.

Sections 10 to 15 work these steps out in full.

9.1How BigQuery runs a query

BigQuery turns a query into a graph of stages. Each stage is run by many parallel workers that use slots, and stages pass data to each other through a shuffle. General, confirm A typical dashboard query has this shape:

  1. Input stage: read the needed partitions and columns of the table. A partition filter decides how many partitions are read; clustering decides how many blocks inside them.
  2. Filter and join stages: apply the row access policy, join to dimensions.
  3. Aggregate stages: a partial aggregate in each worker, then a final aggregate after the shuffle.
  4. Output stage: sort, limit, return.

The plan lists "query plans" as a learning goal of POC F, and asks for partitioning and clustering to be proved by bytes billed (AT-34). Plan

Break-even analysis

14.1The question

For which monthly volume does Enterprise cost the same as on-demand? Plan (F7, step 6) Below that volume on-demand is cheaper; above it, Enterprise.

14.2The two cost curves

With V TiB billed under on-demand and SH = r × V slot-hours under editions:

Cost curves

On-demand: C_od(V) = max(0, V − A) × P_od

Enterprise: C_ed(V) = F + r × k × P_slot × V, with F = B × 730 × P_slot (zero with no baseline)

14.3The break-even point

Set C_od = C_ed for V > A:

Break-even volume

(V − A) × P_od = F + r k P_slot V

Define ρ = r × k × P_slot ÷ P_od, the cost per TiB under editions as a share of the on-demand price.

With no baseline (F = 0): V* = A ÷ (1 − ρ), valid when ρ < 1.

With a baseline: V* = (A + F ÷ P_od) ÷ (1 − ρ).

If ρ ≥ 1, editions cost more per TiB at every volume and there is no break-even.

Three consequences, all Derived:

  1. V* ≥ A always. On-demand is free below the allowance, so editions cannot win below it.
  2. A baseline only moves V* up. The fixed cost F is paid whether or not the queries run.
  3. r decides everything. It is a property of the workload, read from the benchmark, not a price.
